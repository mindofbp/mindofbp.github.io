Research

My research interests are motivated by building fast, reliable, and usable software for conducting physical simultations. Mathematically, I explore the fields of approximation theory, numerical linear algebra, and boundary integral equations. With Prof. Wilber I have used these fields to work on the acoustic scattering problem. We have built a solver for the forward problem using frequency-time-hybrid methods and techniques for nearly-singular quadrature. I am interested in how tools needed for the forward problem can inform techniques in the inverse problem where the goal is to recover information about the bondary of the scatterer. We are actively exploring this area.

Fast NLA and BIEs

I am working with Prof. Heather Wilber on fast numerical linear algebra for acoustic scattering problems solved using boundary integral equations. Our work combines rational approximation and classical BIE analysis to quickly locate scattering resonances across wide frequency bands and solve the scattering problem using a Frequency-Time-Hybrid singularity subtraction method. A manuscript is in preparation.

Laplace BIE Solver

The Laplace problem on a separated domain, normally written with the differential operator, can be rewritten as a boundary integral equation (BIE). This leads to several analytic and numerical niceties. In this project, given Dirichlet boundary data, I solve the resulting integral equation for the unknown density layer and evaluate inside the domain. Specialized quadrature called quadrature by expansion (QBX) is used to improve accuracy near and on the boundary which requires the evaluation of a nearly-singular or singular integral.
